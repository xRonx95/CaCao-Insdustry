RESEARCH LEARNING · ZAMBOANGA DEL NORTE CHALLENGE

Make the process observable.

The supplied industry challenge asks how smallholders and cooperatives can improve post-harvest handling, fermentation and drying to achieve consistent quality and better farmer returns.

The poster reports up to 30–40% downgraded beans. This is a challenge-owner statement, not a measured CaAPs result or a verified pilot baseline.

The working flow

  1. Farmer and harvest provenance: register the farm and harvest, then allocate wet beans to a batch.
  2. Agreed protocol: assigned staff record the facility, buyer/adviser reference, actual fermentation start, minimum duration, turning interval and acceptance limits.
  3. Fermentation and drying: record actual temperature, turning, moisture, drying method and conditions. Reminders compare records with the agreed protocol.
  4. Quality assessment: record a cut-test sample and moisture. Farmers can enter ungraded observations; assigned staff record the final grade and assessment reference.
  5. Storage and handover: storage requires a recorded quality pass and evidence that meets the agreed protocol. Facility handovers record locations and a named recipient.
  6. Sale and learning: record the whole-batch sale and processing costs. Review grades, measurements and costs before adapting the next batch.

What the records mean

  • Measurements are entered manually. No sensor, laboratory or buyer system is automatically connected.
  • Defect categories are mutually exclusive in this prototype: count each sample bean in one category only.
  • Staff-recorded grades are not an independent certification or a guarantee that a buyer will accept the batch.
  • A typed handover recipient is not a digital acceptance signature.
  • Sales less logged post-harvest costs exclude farming costs and unlogged expenses. They are not total farm profit.
  • The downgraded/rejected weight indicator uses the latest effective graded assessment per batch and its weight snapshot at entry. Ungraded batches are excluded; no assessments show “Not assessed”.
  • Corrections preserve original entries. A corrected cost contributes once; the original remains in the export and history.

Research basis

ICCO describes how fermentation and careful drying affect cocoa quality, and notes that fermentation duration varies by bean type. CaAPs therefore records observations and adviser/buyer-agreed targets rather than applying one universal fermentation schedule. ICCO: Harvesting & post-harvest.

DOST-PCAARRD identifies post-harvest skills, bean grading, facility access and awareness of quality standards among Philippine cacao challenges. Software needs to complement equipment, training and qualified review. DOST-PCAARRD: Cacao industry profile.

TESDA’s cacao bean grading competency document is an additional reference for designing a training-led pilot. Confirm current requirements with the cooperative and intended buyer. TESDA: Cacao Bean Grading Level II.

Proposed Research Learning pilot

Recruit one cooperative and 3–5 farmers for a proposed 6–8-week pilot. These are planning assumptions, not confirmed partners or completed fieldwork.

  1. Interview participants and verify the reported problem and existing practices.
  2. Agree the protocol, grading responsibility, measuring tools and data permissions.
  3. Collect baseline information and record comparable batches prospectively.
  4. Review completeness, missed checks, moisture observations, grades, sale price per kg and logged processing costs.
  5. Discuss what changed, revise the workflow and document limitations. Do not attribute price changes to the app without addressing buyer, season, variety and other differences.

Maikling gabay

Itala ang tunay na sukat at petsa ng bawat batch. Ang nakatalagang staff ang nagtatala ng napagkasunduang proseso at pinal na grado. Hindi awtomatikong sertipikasyon ang resulta ng app. Suriin ang aktuwal na benta at gastos bago magpahayag ng pagtaas ng kita.
